Top of Funnel · New Customers

Club-Specific Creative, Turned Into New Customers

The brief

Football Prizes needed to acquire first-time customers across a wide base of football fans on Meta. Rather than running one generic ad, the campaign was structured as eleven parallel ad sets - one per club fanbase, from Liverpool and Arsenal down to smaller clubs like Nottingham - each targeted to its own audience and matched with its own creative for optimal performance.

The approach

Each variant had to walk a line: club-specific enough to feel tailored, without compromising Football Prizes' own brand and colours. The hook in the first three seconds does the heavy lifting - three to four of the club's most desired signed shirts, cut in fast succession. For the Liverpool edit, that's Szoboszlai cutting straight into Steven Gerrard, arguably the club's most iconic player, then Virgil van Dijk, that season's standout performer, then a full squad shirt signed after one of the most famous Champions League finals of all time. Every shirt is a genuine want-item for that fanbase, and all of them land inside the first three seconds.

The same structure repeats across all eleven variants, which is what let one core concept scale without feeling copy-pasted. After the hook, the edit widens into everything else Football Prizes offers - hospitality tickets to the Premier League's biggest fixtures, shown through club-relevant stadium imagery (Anfield for Liverpool, Stamford Bridge for Chelsea, and so on), plus the custom-built, custom-designed frames that set Football Prizes apart from other memorabilia competitors. Each edit closes on a fully customisable end frame, leaving room to land whatever final message the campaign needed to get across to the fan base.

The result

Liverpool was the standout segment - the single largest driver of volume at 3,225 purchases and 13.6× ROAS - while the campaign as a whole has stayed live and profitable since April 2026.

Win-Back · 60+ Days Lapsed

Re-Engaging Customers Who'd Gone Quiet

The brief

Target customers who hadn't purchased from Football Prizes in 60 days or more. Rather than one execution, five creative variants were tested against the same audience - three copy-led statics, one video edit, and one static image - to find what actually re-activated a dormant buyer.

The approach

Three angles ran in parallel to win back lapsed customers:

  • Incentive - a discount code exclusive to anyone who hadn't purchased in 60 days or more.
  • Fresh imagery - new art directions never published on Meta before, placing signed memorabilia into the context of a customer's own home and leaning on the newest shirts in stock, from Lamine Yamal to Suarez at his new home with Inter Miami, to fight the creative fatigue of seeing the same ad style on repeat.
  • FOMO - graphics built around the idea that great prizes have already been won while the customer's been away, alongside a wall of memorabilia with one central frame missing, playing on a collector's fear of never having enough.

Formats were mixed deliberately too. Some viewers want to stop scrolling and read an image at their own pace; others respond better to being told what they're looking at by a trusted face, which is where Jeff Stelling came in as the presenter for the video variants.

The result

The best-performing variant, a copy-led execution, hit 7.2× ROAS, while the static-only ad lagged well behind at 1.7× - a clear signal that motion and message beat a flat image for this audience.

Short-Lifespan · Single Product

Built to Sprint, Not to Last

The brief

A live-competition push telling fans the draw is open now - a single, focused ad built to convert on urgency rather than depth of explanation, and a good example of how a standalone product push performs set against the longer-running club campaigns.

The approach

This ad ran to both cold audiences and returning customers in the same flight, so the edit couldn't lean on any prior brand familiarity - the offer needed to land in seconds regardless of whether the viewer had seen Football Prizes before. The hook follows the same principle as the club campaigns: show the most desirable names in the first three seconds. Here that's a motion-graphics-led edit displaying up to six signed, framed shirts at once, with a constant on-screen line reading "Win a signed shirt for 40p!" - making clear that every shirt on screen, including names like Lionel Messi, Neymar Jr and Cristiano Ronaldo, is winnable at that price.

The edit then cuts back and forth between slow-motion close-ups of individual signatures and wider shots of the framed shirts in motion, reinforcing to the viewer that the prizes on screen are genuinely real.

The result

Over a 22-day flight, frequency reached 5.2 across an audience of just over 6,000 people - enough repeat exposure to convert without overexposing - and the campaign closed at 32.3× ROAS, the strongest return of any single-product push in this portfolio.
